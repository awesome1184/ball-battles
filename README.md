# Ball Battles

A dependency-free browser game prototype. The entire game currently lives in index.html; there is no build step, package install, or backend.

## Run in Codespaces

Open this repository in a Codespace, then run:

    python3 -m http.server 8000 --bind 0.0.0.0

Open the forwarded port 8000 in the Ports panel. The game is served from the repository root.

## Publish with GitHub Pages

1. Open Settings → Pages for this repository.
2. Under Build and deployment, choose Deploy from a branch.
3. Select branch main and folder /(root), then save.
4. Once GitHub Pages finishes publishing, open:

https://awesome1184.github.io/ball-battles/

No GitHub Actions workflow is required for this setup.

## Visual direction

The live page follows the visual guide in `DESIGN.md`: a flat blue backdrop, cream paper panels, ink outlines and offset shadows, flat red/green corners, a ticket-style prediction slip, sentence-case copy, and pixel-art weapon previews from the Pixor CC0 weapon library, with local fallback sprites if the hosted images fail to load. All interface and arena text uses a clear system sans-serif font for readability. The battle engine remains in the standalone `index.html`; the `/ui` scaffold is a reference for the design and future extraction, not a replacement runtime.

## Earclacks-inspired fight presentation

The battle stage uses a square arena, compact matchup header with weapon icons, larger weapon-specific stat readouts, and distinct attack-hit indicators. Fighters now actively steer horizontally according to weapon-specific movement profiles; successful melee hits add a short forward lunge. Bow maintains a ranged gap; its grip is drawn just beyond the orb's front edge, its limbs stay perpendicular to the firing axis, and visibly larger arrows travel strictly along the bow's current rotation without auto-aim or target-leading. Each volley starts with three arrows and gains one arrow per landed hit with no upper cap. Other weapons close to different preferred distances. This is a movement and combat presentation change only; daily coins, predictions, upgrades, simulation tools, records, and the battle engine remain in place.

## Weapon art

The battle arena loads front-view pixel-art PNGs from [Pixor's free weapons library](https://pixor.art/library/weapons/), whose library assets are released under **CC0 1.0**. Used sprites: [sword](https://pixor.art/library/files/sword/sword.png), [dagger](https://pixor.art/library/files/dagger/dagger.png), [spear](https://pixor.art/library/files/spear/spear.png), [battle axe](https://pixor.art/library/files/axe/axe.png), [war hammer](https://pixor.art/library/files/hammer/hammer.png), [war scythe](https://pixor.art/library/files/war-scythe/war-scythe.png), [shuriken](https://pixor.art/library/files/shuriken/shuriken.png) (used for Chakram), [trident](https://pixor.art/library/files/trident/trident.png), [bow](https://pixor.art/library/files/bow/bow.png), and [lance](https://pixor.art/library/files/lance/lance.png). The existing hand-drawn sprites remain as an offline/load-failure fallback; Duplicator remains unarmed.


## Prototype features

- Daily deterministic weapon matchup with an animated canvas battle. Every fighter starts at exactly 100 HP, including Duplicator's main ball and its full-health clones. All fights use 1.5× weapon rotation. Matches have no time limit or overtime damage: a side wins only when every opposing ball has been eliminated.
- Square 336×336 battle arena with a compact matchup header, small weapon icons, flat circular fighter orbs in weapon-specific colors, fine black outlines, and centered high-contrast HP numbers. Weapon sprites prioritize instantly recognizable silhouettes over surface detail: bold shapes, flat fills, sharp outlines, and at most four opaque colors per sprite (outline, main color, shadow, highlight). Tiny bevels, extra material textures, and decorative fittings are removed. Lance is solid gold with a temporary blue shield only while charging; Duplicator is magenta with a small star marker on each clone.
- Gravity-driven arena physics with strong horizontal pursuit and momentum-driven bouncing. Melee fighters continually close toward opponents instead of backing away when they enter weapon range; Bow alone keeps a looser ranged gap. Fighters retain their velocity through wall and floor rebounds, with small deterministic angle changes to keep trajectories varied. Ball-to-ball collisions are elastic, so body contact creates visible rebounds rather than damping everyone to a stop. Bow projectiles test every living opponent along their actual path, including Duplicator bodies, while still firing strictly along the bow's current rotation. Successful melee hits produce a brief lunge and a distinct weapon-colored slash, thrust, sweep, or impact cue. The Trident whirlpool pulls over a 165px radius. Hits cause a hidden, fixed 0.05-second impact-frame interruption while body movement continues. All weapons rotate 1.5× faster than the prior tuning; Bow retains its additional 4× rotation multiplier, and rotation is capped at 32 radians per second.
- Opposing pixel-art weapons physically clash as they rotate. Parries trigger a gold flash, sparks, brief stagger, and reverse the rotation direction of the involved weapons without pushing the balls. Weapon guards can also catch arrows; Hammer attacks remain unparryable.
- Per-hit weapon growth is temporary for that fight; a new fight starts from each weapon's base combat values.
- Damage growth is additive rather than multiplicative. Weapon-specific movement steers fighters toward their preferred engagement distance, while Bow backs away to preserve a ranged gap. Trident's whirlpool follows its rotating weapon head and actively pulls every nearby living ball toward it.
- Each weapon has only one or two hit-scaling attributes, and the arena footer displays only those attributes: Sword gains +0.7 damage per hit; Dagger gains 6% compounding spin speed per hit; Spear gains +0.5 damage and +0.5× its 96px starting reach (+48px) per hit, with uncapped hit-based reach growth; Axe gains +2.5 percentage points of critical chance and +0.12× critical damage per hit; Hammer starts at 1 spin speed, uses spin speed as damage, winds up at 1.0 spin-speed unit per second, gains +0.5 maximum spin speed per hit, and resets to 1 before winding up again. Hammer attacks cannot be parried; Hammer can parry other weapons, but its own rotation never reverses from a parry or weapon clash; Scythe adds +0.11 poison DPS per hit; Chakram gains 4.75% compounding spin speed per hit; Trident gains +0.5 damage and +35 pull strength per hit while its whirlpool radius stays fixed at 165px; Bow starts with three arrows per volley and gains one additional arrow per landed arrow, without a maximum volley cap; Lance gains +2 charge damage per hit and charges along its weapon's pointing angle, starting at 16px/s and accelerating to 440px/s; Duplicator creates one clone every six team hits, up to six balls. Successful hits cause a hidden, fixed 0.05-second impact-frame interruption. It does not scale with weapon or hit count.
- Daily coin claim, one prediction per daily matchup, and simulated community odds.
- Eleven weapon balls: Sword, Dagger, Spear, Axe, Hammer, Scythe, Chakram, Trident, Bow, Lance, and Duplicator. All ten armed weapons share a 96px base combat reach; Lance's sprite is rendered at 1.28× artwork scale without changing its reach, while weapon-size upgrades raise reach to a hard 102px cap (1.7× the 60px ball diameter), leaving roughly one ball diameter of visible weapon beyond the orb; Spear can extend beyond that cap through its uncapped per-hit growth. Duplicator remains deliberately unarmed. Its main ball gains +0.2 damage per main-ball hit, and a full-health 100-HP clone spawns every 6 team hits (up to 6 balls). Clones deal 0.45 contact damage, spawn from the main ball, and can screen it from nearby attackers. If the main dies, the entire swarm is eliminated.
- Per-weapon, per-stat level pools for base damage, scaling, weapon size, projectile rate, attack speed, and spin speed. Each contribution adds 50 coins to that stat's progress bar; the next level costs 50 × (level + 1)² coins. Stat gains start at 1% or 2% per level, and the gain per new level increases linearly (for example, a 2% stat gains +2%, then +4%, then +6%). The prototype stores these pools locally; global cross-player sharing requires the Devvit backend.
- The daily path is: watch the current daily fight, receive the previous day's bet payout on the next daily visit with a one-time animated win/loss reveal, then upgrade the weapon you backed or open Simulate.
- Starting a new live fight, including a practice fight or matchup batch, immediately cancels the previous live fight without recording an unfinished result. The main fight button remains available during a live fight to restart it.
- Daily fight selection simulates batches of 500 deterministic candidates for the day's weapon pairing. A candidate qualifies only if it ends in a knockout within 120 seconds and reaches at least 2.00 excitement; the highest-scoring qualifying candidate wins, and the exact selected seed is replayed for the viewer. If a batch has no qualifying knockout, further 500-candidate batches are searched, with another batch available to start if the initial search limit is reached. The daily selection status shows the score, threshold, and damage activity. Large weapon-specific combat statistics sit immediately beneath the square arena, colored to match each fighter.
- Excitement scoring is weapon-neutral and uses five-second windows. Each window's intensity is `ln(1 + left-side HP damage) + ln(1 + right-side HP damage)`. The score is 65% of average window intensity plus 35% of the average of the three strongest windows, multiplied by `0.75 + 0.25 × active-window ratio`. This rewards sustained exchanges, gives equal credit to damage from either side, and compresses extreme damage bursts with a logarithm. Only actual HP damage contributes: there are no separate points for hit count, critical-hit labels, parries, clashes, arrow launches, clone spawns, Lance charges, winner identity, or remaining winner HP. Empty windows count against the average; movement and elapsed time alone score nothing.
- Live practice uses your trained favorite weapon against a randomly selected opponent. The Matchup Simulator lets you choose any two weapons and run 10, 25, 50, or 100 seeded fights using the actual battle engine. Starting sides alternate, results include win counts, draw/timeouts, and fight durations, and the test runs do not modify your save.
- The archived 11-weapon round-robin under the 336×336 arena ran before the current Spear/Trident progression changes. Each of the 55 unique pairings ran 10 seeded fights (five starts from each side), for 550 fights total. All 550 finished before the 360-second simulator cap, with no draws or timeouts. The matrix reports wins out of all 10 attempts; treat it as a historical baseline, not a ranking of the current balance. Full results and per-pair durations are in `BALANCE_SIMULATION_2026-11-16-336-arena-10-fights.md`.
- Battle audio uses real OGG recordings from Kenney’s Impact Sounds pack (CC0 1.0), loaded from a CDN on demand; the pack license and source manifest are in `assets/sfx/`. Weapon hits draw from material-appropriate metal, plate, punch, wood, and soft-impact recordings. Clashes and parries use metallic strikes and bell rings; wall rebounds, ball collisions, Bow releases, and knockouts have their own recorded samples. Recordings are decoded once through WebAudio, varied slightly in playback rate and volume, capped at 8 simultaneous voices, and controlled by the existing sound toggle. No generated oscillator or filtered-noise sound effects remain.

## Current prototype boundaries

Coins, upgrades, predictions, and records are stored in the current browser using localStorage. They do not sync between players or devices. The official daily matchup is generated from the date, so players load the same matchup and deterministic fight simulation, but the displayed community percentages are currently demo values rather than a live shared pool. There is no account system or server-side validation yet.
